# Event Ops Systems Sprint — validation kit

Nothing in this file has been sent or published. External use requires Keenan's approval.

## Qualified-buyer filter

A prospect counts toward the ten-exposure test only when they operate events, venues, productions, conferences, or experiential programs and have described at least two disconnected tools, duplicate data entry, unclear handoffs, or stale operational reporting.

## Paid-project application draft

I can turn one of your fragmented event workflows into a working Airtable MVP without attempting a risky all-in-one rebuild. The fixed $950 sprint starts by mapping the current process and its failure points, then delivers up to five connected tables, two native automations, one operator interface, test cases, documentation, and a live handoff. I would first narrow the engagement to the workflow whose duplicate entry or missed handoffs cost the team the most time. If the MVP proves useful, any wider migration is scoped separately.

## Direct validation message draft

I am validating a fixed-scope service for event teams that repeatedly update the same operational information across spreadsheets, forms, email, and chat. The deliverable is a working Airtable MVP for one workflow—not a broad software pitch—with documentation and acceptance tests. If that describes a current problem, would you be willing to share which handoff or duplicate update causes the most trouble? I built the offer, and I am using these conversations to decide whether it deserves further investment.

## Objections to record

- Existing tools are sufficient
- Airtable is not approved or already failed internally
- No owner can maintain the system
- The scope is too narrow
- The scope is too broad
- Price is too high without proof
- Price is suspiciously low for the perceived risk
- Integrations, migration, security, or permissions are mandatory

## Evidence threshold

Continue only with at least two qualified discovery calls or one paid deposit from ten qualified exposures. Do not substitute likes, clicks, compliments, or unqualified replies for demand.
